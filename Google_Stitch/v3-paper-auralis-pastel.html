[ Hypothesis & Central Research Question ] Section 00 // Lab Inquiry

“Why does frontier AI struggle with authentic spatial reasoning in AEC, and in which specific computational inflection points does it deliver undeniable structural and workflow value?”

The architectural engineering industry currently confronts an ontological divide: generative vision models synthesize photographic representations of impossible geometries with high aesthetic coherence, yet fail to reason about the structural physics that would allow those forms to stand.

This experiment maps that divide empirically — testing four frontier models across nine architectural task categories, from regulatory code parsing to IFC topology generation — to produce an actionable decision framework for practicing architects and computational designers.

The AEC industry keeps hearing that AI will change everything. But after months of testing tools across real architectural workflows, the picture is more nuanced than the marketing suggests. This is not a review of any single tool. It is a map of where AI currently performs well, where it struggles, and where the ceiling is structurally low — based on direct experimentation.

[ Spatial Cognition — Strong Performance Domains ] Section 01 // AI Excels

Where AI Works Well

01 / Text & Narrative
Text Generation & Summarization
AI excels at producing first drafts of project descriptions, specification summaries, and competition narratives. The output needs editing, but it eliminates the blank-page problem.
Workflow: Brief + program table → Gemini 2.5 Pro → structured narrative in <30s
~85%
Usable content after light editing
02 / Parametric Code
Code Generation for Parametric Tools
LLMs generate functional Grasshopper C# components, Dynamo Python scripts, and Revit API snippets with reasonable accuracy when prompt includes constraints and I/O formats.
Workflow: Façade panelization rule → Claude → working GH C# component
~70%
Functional on first attempt — debugging faster than writing
03 / Regulatory Search
Normative & Regulatory Code Scanning
AI quickly scans and cross-references building codes, zoning regulations, and accessibility standards. Does not replace a code consultant, but accelerates initial compliance checks.
Workflow: Upload zoning doc → ask setback/FAR questions → structured answer with clause refs
~92%
High when source document provided directly
04 / Spatial Analysis
Image Analysis of Architectural Drawings
Multimodal models identify room types, circulation patterns, and spatial adjacencies from floor plan images — useful for rapid typological analysis across large datasets.
Workflow: 10 social housing floor plans → Gemini Vision → room counts, adjacency & ventilation
~90%
Qualitative. ~86% area estimation without explicit scale bars

[ Latent Solver Code — Structurally Bounded Domains ] Section 03 // Ceiling Analysis

Where the Ceiling is Structurally Low

08 / Visualization
Rendering & Generative Visualization
AI image generation produces impressive concept visuals but lacks architectural precision. Fenestration patterns are random, proportions are approximate, structural logic is entirely absent. The images look good but are architecturally meaningless.
Verdict
Useful for mood boards and early concept communication. Not for design development.
09 / Site Intelligence
Site Analysis Automation
AI can process GIS data, solar studies, and climate information, but the interpretation of site-specific constraints — views, adjacencies, cultural context — remains fundamentally human work.
Verdict
Good for data compilation and preprocessing. Poor for design judgment and contextual reading.
BENCHMARK NOTE — These limitations are not model-size problems. Current transformer architectures lack the spatial reasoning primitives required for reliable geometric computation. Scaling does not solve paradigm mismatches.
[ Physical Tectonics — Empirical Synthesis ] Section 04 // Core Learnings

What I Have Learned

01
AI is strongest at text and code, weakest at geometry and coordination.

This maps directly to how transformer architectures work: they excel at sequential pattern matching and struggle with spatial relationships. The architecture of the model predicts the architecture of its failures.

02
The most productive use of AI in AEC is acceleration, not replacement.

Using AI to draft, search, and generate first versions — then applying architectural judgment to refine — produces the best results. The hybrid workflow consistently outperforms either pure-AI or pure-human approaches.

03
Tool-specific workflows matter more than general capabilities.

A well-crafted prompt pipeline for a specific task — extracting room adjacencies from floor plan images — outperforms any general-purpose "AI architect" tool. Specificity is leverage.

04
The gap between demo and production is enormous.

Most AI demos in AEC show cherry-picked results. Reproducibility at production quality remains the central challenge. Test with your actual projects, not curated showcase files.

[ Bibliography & Synthesis ] Section 05 // Conclusion

Conclusion & Next Steps

AI works in AEC where the task is linguistic, pattern-based, or combinatorial. It fails where the task requires spatial precision, cross-document coordination, or domain-specific judgment that cannot be reduced to pattern matching.

Practical Strategy (20 / 80 Rule)

Identify the 20% of your workflow that is text-heavy, repetitive, or search-intensive, and apply AI there. Leave the rest to the tools and judgment that architecture demands. The 80% that remains human is not a limitation — it is the definition of the discipline.
